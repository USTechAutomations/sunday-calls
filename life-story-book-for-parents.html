A life-story book for your parents or grandparents

A life-story book collects one person's memories in their own words, kept on paper so the family can hold it. It might cover the house they grew up in, the work they did, the people they loved, the hard years, and what they hope you remember. This page is for anyone thinking about making one for a parent, and it applies to a grandparent just as well.

Three ways to make one

Write it yourself

You sit with your parent, ask questions, and write or type what they say. It costs the least money and gives you the most control. It also takes the most of your time, and it needs someone to keep going after the first enthusiastic week. Our free interview kit gives you 48 questions to start with.

A question-by-email service

Some services send your parent a question every week by email or text. The storyteller answers, and the answers are gathered into a printed book. This suits a parent who likes to write or type, or one who is happy to record a voice reply on their own schedule. Check each service's own site for how answers are given, since options differ.

Recorded interviews

Here someone asks the questions aloud and the answers are recorded, then turned into pages. Some services do this by phone. This suits a storyteller who would rather talk than write. Sunday Calls is one of these: an AI interviewer, not a human, phones once a week for eight weeks.

Which suits whom

  • Your parent loves to write: a question-by-email service, or writing it yourself.
  • Your parent talks easily but will not type: recorded interviews.
  • You have the time and want it personal: sit down together with the kit.
  • You live far away and cannot visit weekly: a phone-based format fits the distance.
