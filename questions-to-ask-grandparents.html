50 questions to ask your grandparents about their life

Most life-story tools hand someone a list of questions to answer in writing, by email or inside an app. For many grandparents that is harder than it sounds: typing is slow, apps are unfamiliar, and a blank box on a screen is not inviting. A phone call is different. They only have to answer, the way they have answered a phone their whole life. Below are 50 questions you can ask yourself, on a call or in person, grouped into eight parts of a life.

Childhood

  1. What is the earliest memory you have from childhood?
  2. What did your childhood home look like from the front door?
  3. What games did you play with the other kids on your street?
  4. What was your favorite meal as a child, and who made it?
  5. What did a normal school day look like for you?
  6. Did you have any chores, and which one did you dislike most?
  7. What did your childhood bedroom look like?

Family

  1. What do you remember most about your own parents?
  2. What was your grandmother or grandfather like, if you remember them?
  3. How many brothers and sisters did you have, and where did you fall in the order?
  4. What is a family tradition from your childhood that has disappeared?
  5. Who in the family made you laugh the most?
  6. What is something your mother always said that stuck with you?
  7. What is something your father taught you without meaning to?

Work

  1. What was your first job, and how much did it pay?
  2. What did a normal day look like at your longest job?
  3. Who was the best boss or coworker you ever had, and why?
  4. What is something about your work that most people never saw?
  5. What was the hardest physical work you ever did?
  6. Did your work change much over the years you did it?

Love

  1. How did you meet the person you married or loved most?
  2. What do you remember about your wedding day, or the day you decided to commit?
  3. What was your first date like?
  4. What is something about your partner that made you fall for them?
  5. What is the best piece of advice about love you ever received?
  6. What has kept you and your partner close over the years?

Hard times

  1. What is the hardest year of your life, and what got you through it?
  2. Was there a time you lost someone close to you? How did you cope?
  3. Did you live through a war, a depression, or another hard national moment? What was it like for you?
  4. What is a mistake you made that taught you something important?
  5. Was there a time you had to start over? What did that look like?
  6. What did you do on the days that felt impossible?

Beliefs

  1. What do you believe in most strongly, and how did you come to believe it?
  2. Did your beliefs change as you got older? How?
  3. What does faith, or the absence of it, mean to you?
  4. What do you think happens after we die?
  5. What rule have you lived by your whole life?
  6. What do you think makes a person good?

Joys

  1. What is a moment you would live again if you could?
  2. What makes you laugh out loud, even now?
  3. What is the happiest day you can remember?
  4. What small thing brings you joy on an ordinary day?
  5. What is a trip or place that meant the most to you?
  6. What is something you are proud of that most people do not know about?

Messages for the future

  1. What do you want your grandchildren to know about you?
  2. What advice would you give to a great-grandchild you will never meet?
  3. What do you hope people remember about you?
  4. What is a lesson you learned the hard way that you would like to pass on?
  5. What do you want your family to know you loved most?
  6. If you could leave one sentence for the family, what would it say?

You do not need every question, and you do not need to ask them in order. The best call follows whatever the person actually wants to talk about.
