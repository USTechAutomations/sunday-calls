50 questions to ask your parents about their life

A written list of questions asks a parent to sit down and type out answers, in an email or an app. Many parents put that off, or never open it at all. A phone call asks less of them: they just pick up and talk, the way they always have. Below are 50 questions you can ask on a call, grouped into eight parts of a life.

Childhood

  1. What is the first house or apartment you remember living in?
  2. What did you and the neighborhood kids do after school?
  3. What was dinner like in your house growing up?
  4. What subject did you love in school, and which one did you dread?
  5. What did you want to be when you grew up, and why?
  6. What is a rule in your childhood home you never understood until later?
  7. What did your bedroom look like as a kid?

Family

  1. What was it like being the oldest, youngest, or middle child, if you had siblings?
  2. What did your own parents do for a living?
  3. What is something your mother did that you now do yourself?
  4. What is something your father did that you now do yourself?
  5. What holiday from your childhood do you miss the most?
  6. Who was the relative everyone in the family talked about?
  7. What is a story about our family you have never told me?

Work

  1. What was your very first paying job?
  2. What job taught you the most about who you are?
  3. Was there a job you wanted but did not get? What happened?
  4. What is something about your career that surprised you?
  5. Who did you learn the most from at work?
  6. What would you have done differently in your working life?

Love

  1. How did you and the person you married first meet?
  2. What made you decide this was the person you wanted to build a life with?
  3. What is a hard year you and your partner got through together?
  4. What is something you wish you had said to them sooner?
  5. What does a good partnership look like to you?
  6. What is the best advice you would give me about love?

Hard times

  1. What is the hardest thing you have ever had to do as a parent?
  2. Was there a time money was tight? How did you handle it?
  3. What is a loss that changed you?
  4. What did you do when you did not know what to do?
  5. What is a fear you had raising me that I probably never knew about?
  6. What got you through the hardest stretch of your life?

Beliefs

  1. What do you believe about raising children that you did not believe before you had one?
  2. What does faith, or the absence of it, mean in your life?
  3. What is a value you tried hardest to pass on to me?
  4. What do you think makes a family strong?
  5. What rule have you never broken?
  6. What do you think matters most at the end of a life?

Joys

  1. What is your favorite memory of me as a child?
  2. What is a moment as a parent you would relive if you could?
  3. What still makes you laugh after all these years?
  4. What is a small daily thing that makes you happy?
  5. What is a trip or place you would go back to in a heartbeat?
  6. What are you proudest of, in yourself or in our family?

Messages for the future

  1. What do you want me to know that you have never said out loud?
  2. What do you hope for me, more than anything?
  3. What do you want your grandchildren to know about you, if you have them?
  4. What is the one lesson you most want to pass down?
  5. What do you want to be remembered for?
  6. If you wrote one sentence to the family to keep forever, what would it say?

You do not need every question, and you do not need to ask them in order. The best call follows whatever the person actually wants to talk about.
