How to record a parent's life story by phone, step by step

You do not need special equipment to do this yourself. You need a phone, a plan, and more than one sitting. Here is the honest version of how to do it.

Pick a time that works for them, not just for you

Late morning or early afternoon is usually easier than evening, when people are tired. Ask which day is best before you pick one for them. A short call people finish beats a long call people dread.

Find a quiet room and a phone that behaves

Turn off the television, close the window if traffic is loud, and ask them to sit somewhere they will not be interrupted. If you are recording on a phone, test the microphone with a thirty-second practice call first, so you are not troubleshooting during the real one.

How to open the call

Say plainly what you are doing and why: you would like to record this conversation so the family has it, and they can say no or stop at any time. Ask one easy, specific question to start, such as what their childhood home looked like. A specific question is easier to answer than an open one like "tell me about your life."

Ask, then get out of the way

Let the silence sit for a few seconds after they finish speaking. People often add the most important part after a pause, once they think you are done listening. Follow up on a detail instead of moving straight to the next question on your list.

How to follow up on what they say

If they mention a name, a place, or a year, ask about it before moving on. "What happened after that?" and "What was that like for you?" work in almost any conversation. Write down names and dates as you go, since they will matter later when you put the story in order.

Keep the recordings safe

Save each call in at least two places, such as your phone and a computer or a drive, and label the file with the date and topic. A recording that only exists in one place can be lost with the phone it was made on.

Turning recordings into a book

Plan on several passes: a rough transcript first, then organizing by theme or timeline rather than call order, then a readable draft, then a final proofread. Photos and captions take real time to gather and place. This step is usually the longest part of the whole project.

Be honest about the work this takes

Doing this well across eight or more calls, then editing hours of conversation into a book someone will actually read, is a real project. It is worth doing yourself if you have the time and want to be the one holding the phone.
