Assessment and Reserve Funding Disclosure Summary: the California form, line by line

California's Assessment and Reserve Funding Disclosure Summary is a short form set out word for word in Civil Code section 5570. Every association that sends an annual budget report sends this form with it. This guide walks through each numbered line in the order the form prints them, quoting the statute, and ends with a filled sample.

The law says the form shall accompany each annual budget report or summary thereof that is delivered pursuant to Section 5300. It also lets an association add to it: The form may be supplemented or modified to clarify the information delivered, so long as the minimum information set out in subdivision (a) is provided.

The heading: the fiscal year

The form opens Assessment and Reserve Funding Disclosure Summary For the Fiscal Year Ending _____. The statute does not say which fiscal year goes here. Some boards write the end of the budget year the report covers; others write the end of the fiscal year now ending, the same date item (6) uses. The planner sets out both readings side by side, picks neither in advance, and the form does not print until the board picks one. The sample below uses the fiscal year now ending. Check the choice with your association's attorney.

Item (1): the regular assessment

(1) The regular assessment per ownership interest is $_____ per ____. It is the dues per home, per month or per year. The statute does not say whether this is this year's amount or next year's; the planner prints the amount for the fiscal year the board picked for the heading. Where dues differ by unit size or type, the form points to a page of an attached summary.

Item (2): assessments already scheduled

(2) Additional regular or special assessments that have already been scheduled to be imposed or charged, regardless of the purpose, if they have been approved by the board and/or members This line lists any extra assessments the board or members have already voted for, with the date due, the amount and the purpose. If there are none, the table shows None.

Item (3): the 30-year yes or no

(3) Based upon the most recent reserve study and other information available to the board of directors, will currently projected reserve account balances be sufficient at the end of each year to meet the association’s obligation for repair and/or replacement of major components during the next 30 years? The statute does not say which money to count. Some boards answer with the money already approved by the board alone; others assume the reserve funding plan is carried out. The two readings can give opposite answers, so the planner shows both and the board picks one before anything prints.

Item (4): what more would be needed

(4) If the answer to (3) is no, what additional assessments or other contributions to reserves would be necessary to ensure that sufficient reserve funds will be available each year during the next 30 years that have not yet been approved by the board or the members? When item (3) is no, this table lists contributions that have not yet been approved by the board or the members. The planner fills the next five years one row each, then one summary line for years 6 to 30, then the total.

Item (5): all major components included

(5) All major components are included in the reserve study and are included in its calculations. The form prints this as a plain statement with no box: the board confirms it is true, and the planner keeps Print off until the board does. Components with more than 30 years of life left may be left out of the calculation if the decision is disclosed (5570(b)(2)).

Item (6): required amount, cash and percent funded

Item (6) asks for three numbers: the amount required at the end of the current fiscal year, the projected cash balance, and the percent funded. The required amount uses one formula from subdivision (b)(4): the current cost of replacement or repair multiplied by the number of years the component has been in service divided by the useful life of the component The percent funded is the cash divided by that amount. The 5570 calculator guide works this formula through on the Department of Real Estate's own example.

Item (7): the next five budget years

Item (7) repeats item (6) for each of the next five budget years, twice: once taking into account only assessments already approved and other known revenues, and once If the reserve funding plan approved by the association is implemented.

The closing note: interest and inflation

The form ends with the interest rate and the inflation rate the figures assume. The budget report rule caps the interest rate: the calculation may not assume a rate of return on cash reserves in excess of 2 percent above the discount rate published by the Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco at the time the calculation was made.
