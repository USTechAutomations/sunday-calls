California HOA budget report deadline: the 30 to 90 day window and the reserve items

California associations send members an annual budget report before each fiscal year ends. The law sets a window, not a single day, and lists what the report contains. This guide gives the window for common fiscal year ends and the reserve items that go in the report.

The window

Notwithstanding a contrary provision in the governing documents, an association shall distribute an annual budget report 30 to 90 days before the end of its fiscal year.

Source: Civil Code 5300(a)

Dates counted as 90 and 30 calendar days before the last day of the fiscal year. Governing documents can set stricter standards for the report's contents; check yours and the delivery rules in Civil Code 5320 and 4040.

The disclosure form travels with the report: the law says it shall accompany each annual budget report.

What the report holds (Civil Code 5300(b), items 1 to 8)

  1. A pro forma operating budget.
  2. A summary of the association's reserves, prepared under Civil Code 5565 (the bold summary).
  3. A summary of the reserve funding plan adopted by the board, with notice that the full plan is available on request.
  4. Whether the board has decided to defer or not undertake repairs of any major component with 30 years or less of life left, and why.
  5. Whether the board expects special assessments for major components, and if so the amount, start date and duration.
  6. How the board will fund reserves: assessments, borrowing, other assets, deferral or other means.
  7. A general statement of how reserves are calculated, including the 5570(b)(4) formula and an interest rate no higher than 2 percent above the discount rate published by the Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco.
  8. Any outstanding loans with an original term of more than one year.

Items 2 to 7 are the reserve items. The full list continues with insurance and other notices; read it in Civil Code 5300.

Why items 1, 2 and 4 to 8 matter for dues

Civil Code 5605(a) says annual increases in regular assessments cannot be imposed unless the board has complied with items (1), (2), (4), (5), (6), (7) and (8) of Section 5300(b), or the members approve. The statute's words:

Annual increases in regular assessments for any fiscal year shall not be imposed unless the board has complied with paragraphs (1), (2), (4), (5), (6), (7), and (8) of subdivision (b) of Section 5300 with respect to that fiscal year, or has obtained the approval of a majority of a quorum of members, pursuant to Section 4070, at a member meeting or election.

Source: Civil Code 5605(a)

Increases above 20 percent, or special assessments above 5 percent of budgeted gross expenses, need a member vote under 5605(b), except in the emergencies listed in Civil Code 5610.

Putting the reserve items together

The planner prints fill-in blocks for items 3 to 7 with the board's numbers placed in them, the bold summary for item 2, and the disclosure form. The board writes the reasons and adopts the plan at an open meeting, as Civil Code 5560 describes.
