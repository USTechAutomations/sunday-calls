Civil Code 5570 calculator: the reserve amount the form asks for, worked step by step

Item (6) of the Assessment and Reserve Funding Disclosure Summary asks for the reserve amount required at the end of the fiscal year and the percent funded. Civil Code 5570(b)(4) gives the one formula to use. This page works it by hand on the example printed in the Department of Real Estate's Reserve Study Guidelines (RE 25, August 2010, written before the 2014 renumbering of these laws), Exhibit 5.2.

The formula in the law's words

For the purpose of the report and summary, the amount of reserves needed to be accumulated for a component at a given time shall be computed as the current cost of replacement or repair multiplied by the number of years the component has been in service divided by the useful life of the component.

Source: Civil Code 5570(b)(4)

So for each component: cost now, times years in service, divided by useful life. Add the components up and that total is the amount the form calls required. The same subdivision ends: This shall not be construed to require the board to fund reserves in accordance with this calculation.

The worked example

Three components, $22,000 in reserves and 35 homes:

ComponentCost nowUseful lifeYears in serviceCost × years ÷ life
Painting$10,00053$6,000
Paving$14,00074$8,000
Roofing$30,0001511$22,000
Total required$36,000

Percent funded: $22,000 ÷ $36,000 = 61.11 percent. Civil Code 5570 does not say how to round. This planner shows percent funded to one decimal, so its item (6) reads 61.1 percent; as a whole number that is 61 percent.

Shortfall per home: ($36,000 − $22,000) ÷ 35 = $400. That per-unit figure goes in the bold reserve summary that Civil Code 5565 asks for.

Points that change the answer

  • Years in service count from the last time the component was replaced, not from when the buildings went up.
  • The cost is today's cost to repair or replace, not the price paid years ago.
  • The amount is for the end of the current fiscal year. Studies that show the figure for the end of next year can give a different number.
  • Components with more than 30 years of life left can be left out if the decision is disclosed, under 5570(b)(2).

What percent funded is, and is not

Percent funded here is a ratio the form asks for. California sets no percent funded that an association must reach, and the formula itself does not require the board to hold that much. The form also has a line for an alternate, generally accepted method, If an alternate, but generally accepted, method of calculation is also used, the required reserve amount is $____.
