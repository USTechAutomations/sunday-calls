Questions boards ask about Roof and Road

Short answers, with the law's own words where it matters.

Is this a reserve study?

No. It is a planner for the board's yearly review and the reserve parts of the budget report. It does not inspect anything. For most associations the law asks for a visual inspection and full study at least once every three years.

Can a board do its own yearly review?

The law says the board shall review this study, or cause it to be reviewed, annually and does not name a required credential for the yearly review. The Department of Real Estate's Reserve Study Guidelines (RE 25, August 2010, written before the 2014 renumbering of these laws) says a board may decide to produce one or more of these products by itself.

What is the catch with doing it ourselves?

The same guide (RE 25, August 2010) warns that boards could lose the personal indemnity that comes from relying on professional advice if they choose to undertake their own study. Because of this, you should consider seeking legal advice before proceeding.

Will our lender or insurer accept it?

We do not know. We make no claim that any lender, insurer or loan program accepts a board-prepared plan.

Where do the costs come from?

From you: your bids, invoices or last study. The planner ships with typical useful-life ranges and their sources, not prices.

Why might our percent funded differ from our study's?

Studies make a few choices the law leaves open: whether the figures are for the end of this year or next year, how years in service count after a replacement, which calculation method is used, and how interest is counted. Type your study's figures into “Check against my last study” and the planner shows its own figure under each choice, so you can see which one explains the gap.

Does the "required" amount mean we must hold that much?

No. The law says the calculation shall not be construed to require the board to fund reserves at that level.

What about the new rules passed this year?

A 2026 law (AB 2050) adds new reserve duties from 1 January 2032, including a minimum yearly contribution and, for some associations, a 15% transfer. None apply now. The planner shows our estimate of that minimum contribution, labelled “from 1 January 2032”. More on the 2032 rule guide.

What interest rate can we assume?

No more than 2 percentage points above the discount rate published by the Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco on the day you do the calculation. The planner shows the rate we last checked (4.00% primary credit on 5 Oct 2026, Federal Reserve H.15) and lets you change it.

Is our data private?

Yes. The planner runs in your browser and sends nothing to us. Your numbers stay on your computer, in your browser or in the file you download.
