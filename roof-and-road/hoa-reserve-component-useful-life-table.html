HOA reserve component useful life table: 70 common components and their sources

A reserve plan needs a useful life for each major component. This table lists typical useful lives for 70 components common in California associations, with the published sources each range comes from. It carries no costs: those come from the association's own bids, invoices or last study.

Where the ranges come from

  • The California Department of Real Estate's Operating Cost Manual (RE 8) and its addendum.
  • Fannie Mae's expected useful life table for property condition reports.
  • One California reserve firm's sample study (a single example, not a published table).

A range shows the lowest and highest figure across the sources listed for that row. Where one source gives a figure, the range is that figure.

Using the table

The Department of Real Estate's Reserve Study Guidelines (RE 25, August 2010, written before the 2014 renumbering of these laws) points out that Useful life estimates vary considerably from manual to manual, so consulting more than one manual may minimize the risk and asks that the source of each figure be named. Climate and upkeep move real lives up or down; a south-facing wall in full sun weathers faster than a shaded one.

Components with more than 30 years of life left can be handled two ways: Components with an estimated remaining useful life of more than 30 years may be included in a study as a capital asset or disregarded from the reserve calculation, so long as the decision is revealed in the reserve study report and reported in the Assessment and Reserve Funding Disclosure Summary.

Since 2025 the law also counts some utility lines as major components: “major components” includes gas, water, and electrical service to the extent that the association is responsible for repair or replacement of those lines pursuant to Section 4775.

Years in service count from the last replacement. With a useful life and years in service for each component, the 5570 formula gives the required amount for the disclosure form.

Statute text: Civil Code 5570 and Civil Code 5550.
