How the calculator works it out

For each component it takes the cost now, multiplies by the years in service, and divides by the useful life. That is the formula in the statute: the current cost of replacement or repair multiplied by the number of years the component has been in service divided by the useful life of the component Adding the rows gives the required amount. Percent funded is the reserves on hand divided by that amount. The shortfall per home is the required amount minus the reserves, divided by the number of homes, the way Civil Code 5565(d) describes it: The current deficiency in reserve funding expressed on a per unit basis.

Years in service above the useful life are counted as the full useful life, since the component is then fully due. A row with no cost is left out.

Reading the result

The percent is a ratio the form asks for, not a pass mark. California sets no percent funded that an association must reach, and the statute adds that the formula shall not be construed to require the board to fund reserves in accordance with this calculation

Percent funded figures from different studies often differ because of choices the law leaves open: the date the figure is for, how years in service restart after a replacement, and the method used. The full planner shows its own figure under each choice next to the one in your last study.

What the free calculator leaves out

  • The 30-year projection and its lowest year.
  • The other lines of the disclosure form, including the 30-year yes or no and the five-year table.
  • The bold reserve summary and the funding schedule.
  • More than three components, interest and inflation.
