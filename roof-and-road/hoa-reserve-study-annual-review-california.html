Will California's 2032 reserve rule catch your HOA? Check your 30-year low point.

A 2026 law, AB 2050, changes California's reserve rules from 1 January 2032. None of its new duties apply now. What it looks at is simple to check today: the lowest point your reserve balance reaches over the next 30 years.

What changes on 1 January 2032

AB 2050 was signed in 2026. The current reserve study section stays as it is until 2032; the bill adds to it: This section shall remain in effect only until January 1, 2032, and as of that date is repealed. A new version of Civil Code 5550 takes over on 1 January 2032. Among other things, the study then has to show The minimum reserve contribution level to prevent the projected association reserve account balance from falling below zero over the following 30 years.

A new section, 5552, adds what happens if the balance is projected to go below zero:

If an association’s reserve account balance is projected ... to fall below zero at any time over the following 30 years, the association shall transfer a minimum of 15 percent of its gross annual budget to its reserve account each year until its reserve account balance is no longer projected to fall below zero.

Source: AB 2050 (2026), new Civil Code 5552(b), operative 1 January 2032

The same section adds a reserve funding special assessment in some cases, and limits it to once every nine years. SB 1238, a separate 2026 bill on reserves, was vetoed and is not law (bill history).

The 30-year low point

The low point is the smallest year-end reserve balance in a 30-year projection: start from this year's balance, add each year's contributions and interest, take away each year's repairs and replacements, and find the lowest year. If that figure is below zero in any year, the board’s reading of new section 5552(b), quoted above, is that from 1 January 2032 the association would make the yearly transfer the section describes, until the balance is no longer projected to go below zero; the board confirms that reading with its own adviser. Today the same figure already answers item (3) of the disclosure form, which asks whether projected balances will be sufficient each year for the next 30 years.

What Civil Code 5550(a) asks today

Every year: The board shall review this study, or cause it to be reviewed, annually and shall consider and implement necessary adjustments to the board’s analysis of the reserve account requirements as a result of that review.

Every three years, for most associations: At least once every three years, the board shall cause to be conducted a reasonably competent and diligent visual inspection of the accessible areas of the major components The test is whether the replacement value of the major components is at least half the association's gross budget, not counting the reserve account. When it is, the board arranges the three-year inspection; the yearly review sits in between.

Who may do the yearly review

The statute names no credential for the yearly review. The Department of Real Estate's Reserve Study Guidelines (RE 25, August 2010, written before the 2014 renumbering of these laws) says An association board may contract for the preparation of Physical Analysis, Funding Analysis, and operating budget by professionals, or it may decide to produce one or more of these products by itself.

The same guide gives a warning that goes with it: Boards of Directors should consider the fact that they could lose the personal indemnity that comes from relying on professional advice if they choose to undertake their own study. Because of this, you should consider seeking legal advice before proceeding. Boards that do their own review weigh that warning.

What to gather for the check

  • The component list from the last study, with each component's cost now, useful life and years in service.
  • The reserve balance at the end of the current fiscal year.
  • This year's reserve contribution and any assessments the board or members have already voted for.
  • An interest rate within the cap in Civil Code 5300(b)(7), and an inflation rate.
