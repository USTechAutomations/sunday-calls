Why it matters this time of year

California law says the annual budget report goes to members 30 to 90 days before the end of the fiscal year, and the disclosure summary goes with it. For a calendar-year association that is between 2 October and 1 December.

Sources: Civil Code 5300, subdivisions (a) and (e).

What you get

  • A 30-year table: money in, money out, interest and year-end balance, with the lowest year marked.
  • The amount Civil Code 5570(b)(4) says to show as needed: each component's cost times years in service, divided by its useful life. Percent funded is your projected cash divided by that amount.
  • Every line of the Assessment and Reserve Funding Disclosure Summary, including the 30-year yes or no, any assessments not yet approved (the next five years, then one summary line), and the five-year table.
  • Where the law leaves a reading open, the planner shows the choices side by side with the law's words, and your board picks. Nothing prints until you have picked.
  • The bold reserve summary, with the shortfall per unit.
  • A funding schedule with the date and amount of each dues or assessment change.
  • A check of the contribution schedule from your last study: type it in and see the lowest balance and the year it happens.
  • Warnings when a plan would raise regular dues more than 20% over last year, or need special assessments totalling more than 5% of the year's budgeted gross expenses — increases that Civil Code 5605(b) says need approval of a majority of a quorum of members, except in the emergencies listed in Civil Code 5610. Your governing documents may set tighter limits.
  • A check on your interest assumption: the law caps it at 2 percentage points above the discount rate published by the Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco.
  • A side-by-side check against the percent funded in your last study, showing which setting explains a gap.
  • Fill-in blocks for the reserve items of the budget report, with your numbers placed in them. You write the reasons.
  • A starter list of 70 components with typical useful lives and their sources. You enter your own costs.

Who it is for, and who it is not for

  • For: California condominium and planned-development associations whose board reviews its reserve study each year.
  • Not tested for stock cooperatives or community apartment projects.
  • Not a replacement for the full reserve study with a visual inspection, which the law asks for at least once every three years for most associations.
  • Not the balcony and exterior stairway inspection, which needs a licensed structural or civil engineer or an architect. You can enter the repair costs from that report as components.
  • Not for commercial or industrial associations, or associations outside California.
