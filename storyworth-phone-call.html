StoryWorth phone call: what its own site says

People type "storyworth phone call" into a search box when they wonder whether a parent can simply talk instead of writing. This page sticks to what StoryWorth's own website said on 29 September 2026, and then explains how a book made from eight weekly phone interviews works.

What StoryWorth's own site says

StoryWorth's site says it sends "weekly questions by text or email". On how the storyteller answers, its site says:

  • "reply by email, record their voice over the phone, or write on our website"
  • "Tell stories over the phone"
  • "Guided interviews or speech-to-text transcription"

So the phone is named on its site as one way to share stories. The last two lines sit under the heading "Upgrade your plan" on its site. Its site also says plans start at $59 and include one hardcover book.

What we could not find

The finer points of the phone route, such as how long an answer can run, who dials whom, and what it adds to the price, we could not find on StoryWorth's own site on 29 September 2026. We state no feature either way. If the phone route matters to you, ask StoryWorth before you pay.

How an eight-call interview book works

Sunday Calls is a different format. An AI interviewer, not a human, phones your parent or grandparent once a week for eight weeks. Your parent does not type, install an app, or open email. They answer the phone.

Every call opens by saying this is an AI interviewer calling for the family member who ordered the book, that the call is recorded so we can write the book, and asking whether now is a good time. Your parent can say no, and we never call again.

The planned subjects follow the arc of a life, and the interviewer follows what your parent actually says, so a call can wander:

  1. Where they grew up and what they remember of that home.
  2. Parents, grandparents, brothers, and sisters.
  3. School and the friends who stayed.
  4. First jobs and the work of their life.
  5. Love, marriage, and the people they chose.
  6. Children and the family they made, or the family they kept.
  7. Hard years and what helped.
  8. What they want the family to remember, and any advice they want written down.

You keep every recording, and the recordings become one printed hardcover book of about 100 pages. Photos are optional, and the way photos are collected is not set up yet. When you order, you confirm that your parent has agreed to receive the calls. We never call a number nobody signed up.

Choosing between the two

Think about your parent rather than the brand. If they like to write, or to dictate on their own schedule, a weekly question service may fit. If they would rather talk with a voice that calls them at a set time, weekly phone interviews may fit. Other services also interview by phone, so it is worth looking at several.
